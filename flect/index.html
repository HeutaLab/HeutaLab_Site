Apps · Flect

Flect.

The room on one screen. Children mirror their iPads to the teacher’s Mac, the way they would to an Apple TV. Up to twenty-four appear side by side. Click one to fill the screen.

Download Flect For the IT team

What it’s for

Showing a child’s work to the class usually means carrying an iPad to the front, or a dongle, or a cable that fits last year’s iPad. Flect makes the Mac you already use the place the iPads go, and whatever lands there goes on through to the class TV or projector exactly as it does now.

It suits the moments a lesson actually needs: one child’s work on the big screen, four at once to compare, or the whole class on the wall while they work, so you can see who is stuck from where you are standing.

How it works

  1. Open Flect on the teacher’s Mac. It appears in the room under a name you choose, such as “Flect – Room 12”.
  2. On the iPad, open Control Centre, tap Screen Mirroring, and choose that name. There is no app to install on the iPad, no account, and nothing for the child to set up. iPhones work the same way.
  3. The iPad appears on the Mac. Click it to fill the screen, click again for the wall of screens.

In a lesson

  • Up to twenty-four iPads at once, side by side, each labelled with its name.
  • Click one to enlarge it, and its sound comes with it.
  • Silence one iPad, or the lot, without touching anyone’s iPad.
  • Ask before an iPad appears. A card offers Show or Not now, and Show All when a class arrives at once. An iPad you have let on is remembered, so tomorrow it just appears.
  • Keep the work. Save a snapshot of a child’s screen as a picture, or record it as a video with its sound, for their book or the newsletter.
  • A four-digit code on the board, if you want one, so only the people in the room can join.

Worth being clear about

What it doesn’t do

Flect receives pictures and sound. It cannot tap, type or lock anything on an iPad, and it cannot fetch a screen a child hasn’t offered. It shows the whole iPad screen, notifications included. Streaming apps that block AirPlay, such as Netflix, will not mirror to it.

It is not a way to watch a class quietly. The child starts the mirroring, and a recording carries a red badge on the picture the whole time it runs.

For the IT team

What it needs from the network

Flect is an AirPlay receiver, so it needs what an Apple TV needs: the iPads and the Mac able to see each other, and Bonjour (mDNS, UDP 5353) passing between them. Many school networks separate iPads from staff Macs, and that is the usual reason nothing appears. Flect has a check built in that says what it can see and what to pass on to you, with a Copy Details button.

The Mac does the work easily: two dozen streams decode about nine times faster than they arrive, using a fraction of one processor core. The network is what runs out first, so for a class-sized wall of screens, put the Mac on wired Ethernet if you can.

There is an installer package for Jamf, Mosyle and the rest. Flect is not signed by Apple yet, so a hand-installed copy asks for an administrator password the first time; pushed through your management tools it usually opens without one.

What you need: a Mac running macOS 14 or later, Apple silicon or Intel, and iPads on the same network. No accounts, no subscription, no cloud. Snapshots and recordings are saved in the teacher’s own Pictures and Movies folders, and nothing about a lesson leaves the Mac.

Questions

The ones people ask first

Does it work with Chromebooks or Windows tablets?

No. AirPlay is Apple’s, so Flect receives from iPads and iPhones, and it runs on a Mac. Nothing else takes part.

Can I see a child’s screen without them knowing?

No, and that is deliberate. The child chooses to mirror, and their iPad says so while it is happening. If you need to see screens unasked, that is a job for your device management system, not for Flect.

Does it record everything?

No. Nothing is recorded until you press record, on one iPad at a time, and a red badge sits on that picture for as long as it runs. Recordings are saved on the Mac, in Movies. Recording children’s screens is a data-protection question for your school, so check your policy before you make a habit of it.

Do the children need an app, or a login?

Neither. Screen Mirroring is already on the iPad. There is nothing to install, nothing to sign in to, and nothing stored per child.

We already have an Apple TV. Does this replace it?

It doesn’t have to. Flect runs on the Mac, and the Mac can go on mirroring to the Apple TV as it does now, so the iPads land on the Mac and the Mac lands on the big screen. Where Flect earns its place is the things an Apple TV cannot do: several iPads at once, a name on each, and keeping a picture or a recording of the work.

Will it work on our school Wi-Fi?

Usually, and where it doesn’t the reason is nearly always that the iPads and the staff Macs are kept on separate networks. Flect’s network check says whether that is what is happening, in plain English, and gives you the wording for IT.

How many iPads, really?

Four by default, up to twenty-four if you want them. Twenty have run at once on a single MacBook Air. The Mac is not the limit; the Wi-Fi carrying two dozen screens is.

Is it really free?

Yes, and open source under the GPL, so anyone can read it, build it or keep it running without us. Schools pay too much for software that does this, and the money is better spent elsewhere. If Flect earns its place in your classroom, you can give back what it is worth to you — how to do that is being set up now.

Where it stands

Early days, in real lessons

Flect is at version 0.8 and changes week to week. It is built alongside real lessons in one school, and every release is on GitHub for anyone who wants to try it. It stands on UxPlay, the open-source work that makes AirPlay possible outside Apple’s own hardware; Flect adds the Mac app around it and gives its own changes back under the same licence.
