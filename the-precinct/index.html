The cast

Four characters, one story. Pick who you’re drawing, then use their look word for word in every prompt. It’s what keeps them recognisable from panel to panel.

Three tiers, two subjects

Characters

Tested on Gemini“A police chief character for a comic book” came back as a modern colour superhero. Adding era, medium and a named artist (1940s film noir, black and white ink, Al Williamson) flipped the whole style in one step.

Basic

One character portrait. Plain background, one pose, one expression, framed as a character reference sheet.

Medium

The same character in a setting. A shared anchor paragraph describes them, and you reuse it word for word.

Advanced

A story of three or more panels. Plan the shot list with an AI as co-writer before you generate anything.

Settings

Tested on GeminiAsk for “an old police station office” and Gemini fills it with named cops, speech balloons and an arrest in progress. For an empty room, say so: no characters, no text, no dialogue, no captions.

Basic

One empty room, as a wide establishing shot.

Medium

Two rooms that must read as one building. Repeat the anchor exactly, and give each room its own concrete layout line. The anchor alone isn't enough.

Advanced

A location sheet: several exterior shots holding one palette and one time of day.

Pick a palette

Noir here is always monochrome, but the 2023 comic wasn’t only yellow. It ran in five palettes. Choose one, put it in every prompt, and end the line with no other colours. When a palette isn’t pinned down, Gemini drifted to sepia in testing.
