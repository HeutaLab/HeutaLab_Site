Teachers

A lesson outline, setup, differentiation and privacy.

Teaching this?

One lesson, five steps, and what to have ready.

Run the first step from the front, then let the room work. Students look and describe on The Precinct, make the picture in the AI image tool your school has approved, and come back here to check it.

A suggested lesson: about 55 minutes

  1. 10min
    Step 1: Look closelyOn The Precinct
    Read one picture together on the Look closely page. Demo shows worked answers; Guide lets the room agree an answer before you reveal it.
  2. 10min
    Step 2: Choose and describeOn The Precinct
    Students open Case 01: choose a character and a palette, and write what they notice.
  3. 10min
    Step 3: Create with AIOn The Precinct, then in the AI image tool
    Get the brief (this is where the workshop code is asked for), paste it into the image tool, make the picture and save it.
  4. 10min
    Step 4: Check and improveBack at The Precinct
    The picture goes next to the brief. What stayed the same, what changed, and which one thing to change.
  5. 15min
    Step 5: Build and shareBack at The Precinct
    The comic builder turns pictures into a page with captions and speech balloons.

Making pictures takes time, and a comic page needs several of them. Plan a second session for the page.

The link for students

heutalab.com/the-precinct/

Students are learning to

  • analyse visual language
  • keep characters and scenes consistent
  • evaluate the difference between what they asked for and what came back

You will see it when they

  • describe a picture before they prompt: what they see, the details, the world around it
  • can say which steps happen here and which happen in the image tool
  • change one thing at a time, and can say why

Before the lesson

  • Choose the AI image tool your school has approved, and check its age and data rules. Students pick it at Step 3 and see only that tool’s instructions.
  • Have a way in ready for the brief and the desk’s compare check: a workshop code from your facilitator, or an AI key saved on each device (see the AI key page). Students are asked for the code at Step 3, not before. Steps 1 and 2 need neither.
  • Work through Case 01 yourself first.
  • Read what this site sends and stores.

Differentiation: three levels, four kinds of case

The level is how hard a case is. Every case has the same five steps. Case 01 Character and Case 02 Location are Rookie, Case 03 Scene is Detective and Case 04 Page is Commissioner; Case 05 is the check on its own, for any picture. Students change case from the case file, and each case keeps its own work.

    What this site sends and stores

    Kept in the browser, and sent nowhere

    • Where a student got to in each case, and their choices, descriptions, ideas and briefs.
    • The pictures they add at Step 4 and in the comic builder, and the differences and changes they write at Step 4.
    • The workshop code and the level it opens.

    It does not follow a student to another device. On a shared computer, use “Start this case again” in the case file, and “Start again” in the comic builder, when a student finishes.

    Sent to the AI service

    When a student presses “Get my brief”, their description and their idea go to a Cloudflare Worker for this site and then to Anthropic’s API. The desk’s optional compare check sends the brief and the descriptions pasted into it the same way. The workshop code is checked by the Worker only. Nothing a student types is kept on the server or shown to anyone else. Pictures are never sent: the site cannot see them.

    With your own AI key, the key and what is typed go straight from the browser to that service (Google, OpenAI or Anthropic) and never to this site.

    Counted

    Either way, the site counts each request to the desk: the step, the level, the image tool, palette and characters picked, whether it worked, how long it took, and a random number for the browser. Never what anyone types, their pictures, their code or their key.

    The image tool

    The picture is made in an AI image tool outside this site, under that tool’s own terms. The Precinct cannot see what is made there.
