Two Add-Ons and a Clean Screen.

22 September 2026

Saving a YouTube video, or just its audio, with every ripper site blocked — and a board that shows the video you chose and nothing else.

This first went out as Edtech Tuesday #39, a weekly newsletter for teaching staff, on 22 September 2026. Lightly edited for the blog.

The short version. A colleague needed a YouTube video for an assembly and every download site was blocked. Firefox and two free add-ons sorted it in ten minutes. One saves the video, or just its audio. The other strips the ads, the comments and the suggested videos off YouTube, so what goes on the board is the video you chose and nothing else. Firefox is now one of very few browsers where both still work.

A teacher came to me last week wanting Pump Up the Volume by M|A|R|R|S for an assembly. An old video, a great track, and no way of getting it off YouTube on the school network.

Why the ripper sites are blocked

Every YouTube ripper site you’ve heard of is blocked here, and not only for copyright reasons. Those sites fetch the video on their own servers and pass it back to you from an address that isn’t YouTube. To the network, that’s traffic leaving for one place and returning from another with no clear account of what it is, so it gets treated as suspicious and stopped.

For a teacher who wants a song, that’s a false positive. It’s still the right block. Those sites are pop-ups, fake download buttons and worse, and I wouldn’t send anyone there.

A browser, not a website

The way round it is to do the download inside your own browser, straight from YouTube, with an add-on. That route has been quietly closing too.

Google’s Chrome store doesn’t allow extensions that download from YouTube. Video DownloadHelper, the add-on I use, says so on its own Chrome listing. And this August, Chrome cleared the last of the older extension standard that the best ad blocker, uBlock Origin, depends on, and Microsoft announced Edge will follow by the end of the year. Firefox’s reply was one line on Bluesky: its support for uBlock Origin isn’t going anywhere.

Brave and Opera still run uBlock Origin, for now. Firefox is the one I’d point you to, because both add-ons below come from Mozilla’s own add-on site and both do the whole job there.

One: Video DownloadHelper

On the Firefox add-ons site, find Video DownloadHelper by vdh. It carries Mozilla’s Recommended badge, meaning it has met their security and performance standards, and it has been listed since 2006.

Install it, open the YouTube video, and click the add-on’s icon in the toolbar. You can save the whole video, or just the audio.

The audio is the one people don’t realise they want. The track for the assembly walk-in. A speech for a listening task. The music under a class performance, without a video pulling eyes off the stage.

Two things to know first. Your first YouTube download will ask you to install the Companion App, a small program that stitches YouTube’s separate picture and sound back into one file. It’s how the add-on works, not a catch, but it is a program on your laptop, so if yours won’t let you install it, that’s a quick word with IT. And the free version gives you three YouTube downloads to start with, then one every two hours. For a lesson, that’s plenty.

Two: uBlock Origin

Find uBlock Origin by Raymond Hill on the same site and install it. The ads disappear straight away, which on its own is worth it in front of a class.

The part that matters more is that it lets you remove whole parts of a page. On YouTube, click the uBlock Origin icon in the toolbar, then the eyedropper. Click the column of suggested videos, then click Create. It’s gone, and it stays gone. Do the same to the comments. If YouTube ever changes its layout, do it again.

Use the eyedropper, not the lightning bolt beside it. The bolt does the same trick, but only until you reload the page.

Now the board shows the video you chose and nothing else. No thumbnail of whatever the algorithm fancies next. No comment you’re reading one second ahead of twenty-two ten-year-olds. That’s an ad blocker doing a safeguarding job, and you only set it up once.

The small print

YouTube’s own terms don’t allow downloading, and part of the reason the ripper sites are blocked is copyright. So keep this to what you’d happily show anyway: a clip for a lesson or an assembly, played in school, not shared on. If a video becomes part of every year’s teaching, it’s worth asking whether a licensed copy exists.

Something to try this week

A clean YouTube for your board

Ten minutes, once.

  1. Install Firefox. If your laptop won’t let you, ask IT.
  2. In Firefox, open uBlock Origin and click Add to Firefox. If the link opens in another browser, copy it into Firefox.
  3. Open YouTube. Click the uBlock Origin icon, then the eyedropper. Click the suggested videos, then Create. Repeat for the comments.
  4. Add Video DownloadHelper the same way.
  5. Find a video you’ll need next week. Save it, or save just the audio.
  6. In the lesson, play the file, not the page. No buffering, no ads, nothing you didn’t pick.
